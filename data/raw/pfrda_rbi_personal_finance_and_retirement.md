# PFRDA & RBI - Personal Finance, Retirement Schemes, and Wealth Building

## What is the National Pension System (NPS) Tier 1 vs Tier 2?
The National Pension System (NPS), regulated by PFRDA, is a voluntary long-term retirement savings scheme:
- **NPS Tier 1**: A mandatory retirement pension account with tax benefits (up to ₹1.5 lakh under Section 80CCD(1) plus an exclusive additional ₹50,000 under Section 80CCD(1B)), subject to lock-in until age 60, after which up to 60% can be withdrawn tax-free and 40% must be converted into an annuity pension.
- **NPS Tier 2**: A voluntary open-access investment account with no lock-in period and unrestricted withdrawals, offering low-cost market-linked fund options across Equity (E), Corporate Bonds (C), and Government Securities (G).

## What is Public Provident Fund (PPF) vs Employee Provident Fund (EPF)?
Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a central government-backed 15-year savings scheme open to all Indian residents, featuring sovereign safety, EEE tax status (Exempt on contribution, Exempt on interest, Exempt on maturity), an annual contribution limit between ₹500 and ₹1,50,000, and annual compounding interest. Employee Provident Fund (EPF) is a mandatory retirement fund for salaried employees in registered establishments where the employee and employer each contribute 12% of basic salary plus dearness allowance.

## What is an Emergency Fund and the 50-30-20 Budgeting Rule?
An Emergency Fund is a liquid cash reserve equivalent to 6 to 12 months of mandatory living expenses (rent, groceries, EMIs, utilities, insurance premiums) kept in high-liquidity instruments (savings accounts, sweep-in FDs, or liquid mutual funds) to cushion against job loss or medical crises. The 50-30-20 Rule is a budgeting framework allocating 50% of monthly net income to essential Needs, 30% to discretionary Wants, and 20% to Savings, Debt Payoff, and Investments.

## How Does Inflation Erode Purchasing Power and How to Beat It?
Inflation is the continuous rise in the general price level of goods and services over time, which decreases the purchasing power of money. For example, at a 6% annual inflation rate, ₹1,00,000 today will be worth approximately ₹55,800 in purchasing power after 10 years. To prevent wealth erosion, investors must allocate a portion of their surplus into growth assets like equities, index funds, and inflation-indexed bonds that deliver post-tax returns exceeding the consumer inflation rate.
