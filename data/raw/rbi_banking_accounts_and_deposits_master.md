# Reserve Bank of India - Master Direction on Banking Accounts and Deposits

## What is a Savings Bank Account vs a Current Account?
A Savings Bank Account is a deposit facility designed for individuals and HUFs to accumulate savings while earning periodic interest (typically 2.5% to 4% per annum, credited quarterly or monthly). It has limits on daily cash withdrawals and commercial transactions. In contrast, a Current Account is designed for businesses, enterprises, and commercial entities requiring unrestricted deposits and withdrawals with overdraft facilities, but it earns zero interest under RBI regulations.

## What is DICGC Deposit Insurance Protection?
The Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC), a wholly owned subsidiary of the Reserve Bank of India, insures bank deposits across all commercial, cooperative, regional rural, and small finance banks. Each depositor in an insured bank is insured up to a maximum of ₹5,00,000 (Rupees Five Lakh) for both principal and interest amounts held in the same capacity and same right across all branches of that bank.

## What are Fixed Deposits (FD) and Recurring Deposits (RD)?
A Fixed Deposit (FD) is a financial instrument where a lump-sum amount is deposited with a bank for a fixed tenure (ranging from 7 days to 10 years) at a guaranteed interest rate. Interest is calculated on a quarterly compounding basis. A Recurring Deposit (RD) allows depositors to contribute a fixed equal monthly instalment for a pre-decided term (ranging from 6 months to 10 years), accumulating interest at rates equivalent to term deposits.

## What is the Senior Citizen Savings Scheme (SCSS) and Senior Citizen FD Rates?
Banks in India provide an additional interest rate benefit (typically 0.50% to 0.75% extra) on Fixed Deposits for senior citizens (individuals aged 60 years and above). The Senior Citizen Savings Scheme (SCSS) is a government-backed retirement savings program offering quarterly interest payouts, high capital security, a maximum deposit limit of ₹30,00,000 (Rupees Thirty Lakh), and tax benefits under Section 80C.

## What is an Inactive or Dormant Account and How to Reactivate It?
Under RBI guidelines, a savings or current bank account is classified as 'inactive' or 'inoperative' if there are no customer-induced transactions (financial or non-financial) for a continuous period of more than two years. To reactivate a dormant account, the account holder must submit fresh KYC documents (Officially Valid Documents) in person or via video KYC at the bank branch. Banks are strictly prohibited from levying any fee for reactivation of inoperative accounts.

## What are Nomination Facilities and Joint Account Operation Modes?
Nomination is a statutory facility allowing an individual account holder to designate a nominee who will receive the deposit balance in the unfortunate event of the depositor's demise. Joint accounts can be opened with operating instructions such as 'Either or Survivor' (either holder can operate the account), 'Former or Survivor' (only the primary holder operates during lifetime), or 'Jointly' (all account holders must sign for all transactions).
