# Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) - Capital Markets and Securities Guide

## What is a Demat Account vs a Trading Account?
A Demat (Dematerialized) Account is an electronic repository managed by central depositories (NSDL or CDSL) that securely stores your shares, government bonds, exchange-traded funds (ETFs), and mutual fund units in digital format. A Trading Account is an account opened with a SEBI-registered stockbroker that allows an investor to place buy and sell execution orders on stock exchanges like the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE).

## What is an Initial Public Offering (IPO) and the Allotment Process?
An Initial Public Offering (IPO) is the process by which a private corporate entity issues fresh shares or offers existing shares for sale to the public for the first time, getting listed on stock exchanges. Retail individual investors apply for IPO shares using UPI Application Supported by Blocked Amount (ASBA), which blocks application money in the investor's bank account until final allotment without debiting funds prematurely.

## What are Dividends, Stock Splits, and Bonus Shares?
A Dividend is a portion of a company's net profits distributed directly to its shareholders in proportion to their shareholding. A Stock Split is a corporate action where a company divides its existing shares into multiple shares (e.g. 1 share of ₹10 face value split into 5 shares of ₹2 face value), increasing liquidity without changing the overall market capitalization. Bonus Shares are additional free shares given to existing shareholders funded out of the company's accumulated reserves.

## What are Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGB) and Government Securities (G-Secs)?
Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) are government securities denominated in grams of gold issued by the Reserve Bank of India on behalf of the Government of India. SGBs pay a guaranteed annual interest coupon of 2.50% on the initial issue price (paid semi-annually), eliminate purity and storage concerns, and offer complete capital gains tax exemption upon maturity (8 years). Government Securities (G-Secs) and Treasury Bills (T-Bills) are debt instruments issued by the central government carrying zero default risk (sovereign guarantee).
