# Reserve Bank of India - Master Direction on Credit Card Issuance and Operational Conduct

## What is a Credit Card Interest-Free Grace Period?
The interest-free grace period (free credit period) is the window between the transaction date and the payment due date during which no interest is charged on retail purchases, ranging from 20 to 50 days depending on the transaction timing within the billing cycle. The grace period is ONLY available if the Total Amount Due from the previous billing cycle was paid in full by the due date.

## What are the Consequences of Paying Only the Minimum Amount Due (MAD)?
The Minimum Amount Due (MAD) is typically 5% of the total outstanding statement balance. While paying MAD protects the cardholder from incurring late payment penalty fees and negative CIBIL default reporting, the entire remaining 95% balance attracts high finance charges (Annual Percentage Rate of 36% to 42% per annum) calculated on an average daily balance basis starting from the exact date of original purchase. Additionally, all new transactions immediately lose the interest-free grace period.

## What is a Cash Advance (ATM Cash Withdrawal on Credit Card)?
A Cash Advance is the withdrawal of physical cash from an ATM using a credit card. Unlike retail purchases, cash advances do NOT have an interest-free grace period; interest accrues from day one until the balance is cleared. In addition, an upfront Cash Advance Transaction Fee (typically 2.5% to 3% of the withdrawn amount, subject to a minimum of ₹300–₹500) plus 18% GST is levied.

## What are International Credit Card Markup Fees and Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC)?
When a credit card is used for foreign currency transactions or on international online websites, banks charge a Foreign Currency Markup Fee (typically 1.5% to 3.5% of the transaction value) plus GST. Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC) allows paying in Indian Rupees (INR) at an overseas merchant or POS terminal, but DCC often applies an opaque conversion markup of 4% to 8%, making DCC significantly more expensive than paying in local currency.

## What is the Chargeback Mechanism and Credit Card Fraud Zero-Liability Policy?
A Chargeback is a formal dispute mechanism governed by card networks (Visa, Mastercard, RuPay) where a cardholder disputes an unauthorized, erroneous, or unfulfilled transaction. Under RBI Master Directions on customer protection, if a cardholder suffers an unauthorized transaction arising from fraud, phishing, or bank system deficiency and reports it to the issuer within 3 working days, the cardholder enjoys Zero Maximum Liability and the issuer must credit the disputed amount within 10 working days.
