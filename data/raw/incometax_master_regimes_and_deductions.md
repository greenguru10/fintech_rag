# Income Tax Department of India - Master Guide on Tax Regimes, Deductions, and Capital Gains

## Old Tax Regime vs New Tax Regime Comparison
Under the Indian Income Tax framework, taxpayers can choose between the Old Tax Regime and the New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC). The Old Tax Regime features higher slab rates but allows extensive statutory deductions (Section 80C, 80D, HRA, LTA, standard deduction, home loan interest). The New Tax Regime offers lower concessional tax slab rates, a higher basic rebate limit under Section 87A (tax-free income up to ₹7,00,000 for salaried individuals with standard deduction up to ₹7,75,000), but disallows most traditional chapter VI-A deductions.

## Key Income Tax Deductions: Section 80C, 80D, 80TTA, and Section 24(b)
- **Section 80C**: Allows deduction up to ₹1,50,000 for investments in PPF, EPF, ELSS mutual funds, Life Insurance premiums, 5-year Tax-Saving FDs, Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY), and principal repayment on home loans.
- **Section 80D**: Allows deduction for health insurance premiums paid for self, spouse, and dependent children (up to ₹25,000) and an additional deduction for senior citizen parents (up to ₹50,000).
- **Section 80TTA**: Allows deduction up to ₹10,000 on savings bank account interest for non-senior citizens (Section 80TTB offers up to ₹50,000 for senior citizens across all deposits).
- **Section 24(b)**: Allows deduction up to ₹2,00,000 per financial year on home loan interest payments for a self-occupied residential property.

## What is Capital Gains Tax (LTCG and STCG) on Stocks, Mutual Funds, and Real Estate?
Capital Gains arise from the sale of capital assets. For listed equity shares and equity mutual funds:
- **Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG)**: Applies when units are held for 12 months or less, taxed at a flat rate of 20% plus cess.
- **Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG)**: Applies when units are held for more than 12 months, taxed at 12.5% plus cess on gains exceeding the threshold exemption limit of ₹1,25,000 per financial year.
For unlisted assets and real estate, holding periods and indexation benefits are governed by revised Finance Act provisions.

## What are Form 16, Form 26AS, Annual Information Statement (AIS), and Advance Tax?
Form 16 is a salary TDS certificate issued annually by employers showing salary breakdown and tax deducted. Form 26AS is a consolidated annual tax statement showing TDS, TCS, and tax collected under your PAN. The Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS) provide a comprehensive view of all financial transactions (dividends, interest, stock transactions, mutual fund sales, high-value purchases). Advance Tax must be paid in quarterly installments if the estimated total tax liability exceeds ₹10,000 in a financial year.
